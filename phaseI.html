INCANTATION Phase I: Visual Essay

Fruiting Bodies

ABSTRACT: Fruiting Bodies engages the visual metaphor of fruit and produce arrangements found in ten different grocery stores located along the walk from my apartment in Ridgewood, Queens to my workplace in Bushwick, Brooklyn. Grocery stores are porous to every community group found in these neighborhoods (and beyond), and fruits hold the power to bring people together, in their cultural histories, seasonality, and optical and gustatory charm.

We begin by looking at fruits in their various arrangements, create forums to share personal histories with fruits, and also use fruits to picturize the chasms that have been carved between marginalized and/or diasporic communities.

In this effort to build coalitions through new languages and fruit-based frameworks, we harness the inherent nutritional power of fruits themselves and work towards suturing the fractured solidarity between marginalized groups.

PROJECT DESCRIPTION: The history of movements to connect marginalized bodies with each other is often connected to the concerted efforts to break up those movements. Is there a spell that can break the cycle? In Fruiting Bodies, we ask how the arrangements of fruits found in grocery stores along my daily commute to work can lend their power to strengthening solidarity between marginalized and/or diasporic communities in our neighborhoods.

The different magnifications of research: How can fruits open up discourse into the city in small fragments, and work towards systemic divisions at larger, even international scales and histories.

At the fruit level, we can study specific traits, such as people’s proclivities, taste, cultural histories, as well as the availability, accessibility, pricing, etc. of the fruits themselves.

As we work towards a local forum of discourse, can we begin by talking about fruits in our personal originary contexts/personal narratives, and then place those stories into a shared dialectical framework? We can begin by sharing what certain fruits hold and mean to us, and arrive at the contemporaneous moment in which there is power and agency to be reclaimed, shared ground to be unearthed, and power brokers to be challenged. At this stage, we can use fruits to visualize our communities and people themselves, and look at how the grocery stores around us open up this visual language.

Zooming out, the project can research and picturize international movements like the Bandung Conference, where newly independent Asian and African nations began working towards collaboration in economic and cultural development, while resisting colonial/neocolonial influence. This can be juxtaposed with research into national agendas like COINTELPRO, that actively broke apart movements by marginalized groups calling for justice, at a time when Asian and Black solidarity in particular was strong in the US.

Finally, we can zoom back in to the community level, where our Latinx community and undocumented neighbors are at constant risk from ICE, and face systemic racial injustice. Often, ICE agents have been found posted outside grocery stores in Bushwick, profiling those entering.

If we are all fruits, how can we look at arrangements of produce to understand possibilities for solidarity movements, and can we use the communalizing activity of sharing fruits as an incantation to set us free from racial injustice, particularly at the neighborhood level?

Consider the postcolonial and post capitalist realities of fruits in grocery stores: -where do they come from? -what determines the selection and variety of fruits available? -how are they priced? -who purchases what? -does everyone eat fruit? -how do they taste? -what are the different ways fruits are consumed/used? -are they nutritional? -what can be gleaned from these realities as we consider difference and imagine connection?
